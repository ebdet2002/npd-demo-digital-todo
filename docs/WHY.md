# WHY

## Customer

Grad students and knowledge workers whose week is split between two very
different kinds of obligation: long, sustained research or writing work that
needs an uninterrupted block of attention, and a constant stream of short,
reactive admin — email replies, form submissions, scheduling, small fixes —
that can happen in any spare five minutes.

## Pain point

Ordinary todo apps treat every task identically: one flat list, sorted by due
date or priority, with no distinction between "this needs two protected hours
of deep thinking" and "this takes ninety seconds between other things." Because
the shallow admin tasks are more numerous and feel satisfying to check off, they
constantly leak into time that should be protected for deep work — a student
opens their todo app to start a writing session, sees twelve quick tasks sitting
right next to it, and clears three of them before they've written a sentence.
The cost isn't the three minutes those tasks took; it's the attention switch
and the twenty minutes of refocusing it takes to get back into the writing. No
mainstream todo app treats this switching cost as the thing to design against —
they optimize for "never forget a task," not "protect the kind of attention this
task needs."

## Novel angle

Every task is tagged **Deep** (needs a sustained, protected block of attention)
or **Shallow** (quick, fine to do in fragments). The app has a **Focus Mode**
toggle that changes what the list even shows, not just how it's sorted:

- **Focus Mode ON**: shallow tasks are hidden entirely. The screen shows only the
  current deep task and a countdown timer, so there is nothing to context-switch
  into.
- **Focus Mode OFF**: the full list is visible, grouped by Deep / Shallow, with
  shallow tasks surfaced for exactly the moments — waiting for a meeting, a
  study-break — they're suited for.

The product's bet is that the categorization itself, not reminders or
prioritization, is what protects focus.
